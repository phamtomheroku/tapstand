# Prompt: source the 5×7 frame and the insert print path

Copy everything below the line into a **new session with your local agent** — one that has a
real browser and can open shopping and supplier sites. It is self-contained; the new session
needs no context from anywhere else.

## Who you're working for

I sell **NFC tap-to-review stands** to local businesses — a customer taps their phone on the
stand and lands on the business's Google review page. The competition (Repuso and similar) ships
one generic frosted acrylic tent to everybody. My whole difference is that mine are **custom per
business and actually look premium**, and that I can turn one around **same day or next day**.

I have a design tool that generates the printed insert. I already print inserts myself on an
**HP OfficeJet Pro 8720** (inkjet, feeds cardstock up to roughly 200 gsm from the main tray).

**NFC tags** — Seritag order, 10 of each:

## The job

Two deliverables. Both go in one written report.

### Deliverable 1 — source the frame in quantity

I need to buy the 5 × 7 holder in real quantities at a price that leaves margin. Find me the best
options and price them.

**What "good" looks like, in priority order:**

1. **Small footprint.** The base must not sprawl. Counter space near a register is contested — a
   deep easel foot or a wide weighted base is a reason for a business to move it somewhere useless.
   Depth at the base under ~55 mm is the target.
2. **Premium feel.** Heft. Thick material (3 mm+ acrylic, or metal, or wood). Polished or flame-
   polished edges, not saw-cut. No visible seams, no cloudy cast, no injection-mould sprue marks.
   If I hand it to a salon owner it should feel like something they'd have bought themselves.
3. **Clean and mostly clear** — the insert is the design, the holder should disappear. Frosted is
   acceptable if it reads as intentional, not as cheap.
4. **The insert slides in and out easily**, so a business can swap it and so I'm not gluing anything
   shut. A top slot or a magnetic two-slab is fine. A frame I have to disassemble is not.
5. **A flat back**, or at least a flat area, so I can apply 3M VHB myself and a customer can peel-
   and-stick it to a wall or counter edge. I supply the adhesive — I don't need it pre-applied.
6. **Bulk availability.** 10 units to start, then 25, then 100+.

**For each candidate, get me:**

- Product name, supplier, and a **link you have personally opened and confirmed loads**
- A photo
- Exact outer dimensions **and base depth** — if the listing doesn't say, say it doesn't say
- Material and thickness
- Price at 1 / 10 / 25 / 100 units, including any bulk breaks
- Shipping cost and **lead time to Oregon, US**
- MOQ
- Whether they'll do **custom** — my logo, a custom size, a custom cut — and what that costs and
  what the MOQ becomes

**Look in all of these places, not just Amazon:**

- Amazon, but treat it as the floor on quality, not the answer
- Wholesale display suppliers: Displays2Go, Buy Bulk Displays, Displays and Holders, Dasher
  Products, Clear-Ad, Marketing Holders, Azar Displays, Deflecto
- Restaurant and hospitality supply: WebstaurantStore, Menu Cover Man, Risch
- Etsy and small makers, for wood/brass/terrazzo — these are where "premium" usually actually lives
- **Alibaba / 1688 / Made-in-China** for custom acrylic and metal fabrication at 100+ — get me real
  quotes from at least three suppliers, with their MOQ and per-unit at 100 / 500
- US laser and CNC shops that will cut custom acrylic to my drawing: Ponoko, SendCutSend, Xometry,
  and any local Portland-area shop you can find. Price a **three-layer stack** (2 mm front plate +
  1.5 mm window spacer + 3 mm back plate, bonded, 6.5 mm total) sized for a 127 × 178 mm insert.
  A laser cuts *through* — it cannot mill a pocket — so the slot has to come from the spacer layer,
  not from machining a groove.

### Deliverable 2 — the insert print path

I print on the OfficeJet Pro 8720 now. That's fine for one-offs and it's what makes same-day
possible. I want to know what I move to when I'm doing volume, and what stock makes a home-printed
insert look bought rather than printed.

Find me:

- **Cardstock that runs through an OfficeJet Pro 8720** and looks premium at 127 × 178 mm. I want
  specific products with links — brand, weight in gsm and lb, finish (matte / satin / soft-touch),
  and a confirmation the 8720's tray actually takes that weight. Check HP's stated media specs for
  this exact model rather than guessing.
- **Whether the 8720 does borderless at 5 × 7** and on what media types. My designs bleed to the
  edge, so if it can't, I need to know now — the fallback is printing oversize and trimming, which
  means I also need a trimmer recommendation that holds a straight edge at this size.
- **Print shops that take a press-ready PDF at exact trim size** and will do short runs — 25 to 250
  — of 127 × 178 mm cards on heavy stock. Local Portland-area shops and online (Moo, Vistaprint,
  4over, PrintingCenterUSA, Jukebox). Price 25 / 100 / 250, and get turnaround times, because
  next-day is the promise I'm selling.
- **A finishing option that reads premium**: soft-touch lamination, spot UV, painted edges, rounded
  corners. What each costs at 100 units and who does it.

## How to work

**Use a real browser.** Most of these sites return 403 to plain `curl` or a non-browser agent.

**Open every link before you put it in the report.** This is the part that matters most to me. I
have been handed lists of links before where half of them 404'd, and it made the whole report
worthless because I then couldn't trust the half that did work. If a page doesn't load, doesn't
exist, or has changed to a different product, either find a working replacement or leave it out and
say you left it out.

**Report honestly.** If you found four good frames and not ten, give me four and say four. If a
supplier's pricing is behind a quote form and you couldn't get a number, say that rather than
estimating one. Don't fill gaps with plausible-sounding prices, part numbers, or lead times — a
short honest report is worth more to me than a long one I have to re-verify.

**Rank, don't just list.** End with a clear recommendation: the one frame you'd buy 25 of, and why,
and what the landed per-unit cost is. I'd rather argue with a real opinion than pick from a table.

## Format

A single markdown file. A table for the frame candidates, a table for the print options, then a
short recommendation section at the end. Flag anything you were unsure about in its own list at the
bottom so I know exactly what still needs checking.
